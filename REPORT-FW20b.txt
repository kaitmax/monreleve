FACT 8
--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ROW (docs/regulatory-sources.md, D8):

  A family member residing in France who has no professional activity of
  their own and no pension or annuity from the Swiss scheme alone does
  not exercise a separate option: their health cover follows the option
  the worker exercised. A spouse who works - in France or in Switzerland
  - falls outside this and has their own affiliation.
  Verified: CLEISS, 16.09.2026.

THE GUIDE PARAGRAPH is written accented in content/guides and is not
transliterated here (R168). In English: a family member resident in
France who carries on no professional activity and draws no pension or
annuity from the Swiss scheme alone does not exercise an option of their
own, and their cover follows the choice the worker made. Then, in its own
warning box:

  the condition is half the rule. It holds only for a relative with no
  professional activity of their own and no Swiss-only pension. A spouse
  who works - in France as in Switzerland - is outside it, has their own
  affiliation, and is not covered by your choice.

WHY THE QUALIFIER IS LOAD-BEARING, in the operator's terms: the failure
direction. A two-earner household reading "your family follows your
choice" without it concludes the working spouse is covered, does nothing,
and finds out at a hospital desk. An omission that produces a wrong
belief is not a smaller claim than a wrong sentence - here it is the more
expensive one.

So it is an Avertissement rather than a subordinate clause, and the test
asserts the qualifier is IN THAT ELEMENT rather than merely in the
section. That distinction was found by planting: deleting the warning box
left both phrases standing in the lead paragraph, and the first version
of the test passed on a page that had lost the one element a skimming
reader stops at.

FACT 6 BECAME TWO ROWS, NOT ONE. The operator's steer was "probably a
row"; the page argued for two, on R158's own test - a source key
identifies a fact, not a URL:

  D9  droitOptionFiling - where it is exercised, by status: the competent
      cantonal body of the canton of work; or Institution commune LAMal,
      Olten, for a holder of a Swiss pension alone. Part 6 by the CPAM of
      the French place of residence, the whole within the window.
  D10 droitOptionForm - the instrument, by NAME, which CLEISS marks as
      being updated.

They have DIFFERENT ROT PROFILES. CLEISS can reissue the form under a new
reference without the cantonal bodies moving; Olten's address can change
without the form changing. One key covering both would leave `claims`
unable to tell "a pensioner files at Olten" from "the instrument is <the
form's name>" - exactly the state missionCapDays was in before R158.

R167 IS ENFORCED, NOT JUST OBSERVED. The article names the instrument and
links no PDF, and tells the reader to ask the body they file with for the
current version rather than a third-party site, THIS ONE INCLUDED. A
planted link to the juin2019 PDF turns the assertion red.

AND THE TEST DOES NOT TYPE THE FORM'S NAME. It reads it out of the
guide's own droitOptionForm claim and looks for that in the served body,
so renaming it in frontmatter carries the test along and typing a variant
into the prose fails. The first version typed it, which is the defect
R167 exists to survive, inside the test written to enforce R167.


THE TWO CARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
FD12: manual and automatic are MECHANICALLY IDENTICAL, and the same green
check on every line INCLUDING the line about needing access to the device
position. A requirement is not a drawback.

WHAT KEEPS THEM IDENTICAL, in order of how hard it is to break:

  1. ONE TYPE. `LoggingMode` has no `recommended`, no `badge`, no `tone`
     field. There is no way to express "this one is better", so no future
     edit can add the distinction without adding the field - which is a
     visible decision rather than a styling tweak.
  2. ONE ARRAY, ONE MAP, ONE CLASS. Both cards render from
     APPLICATION_COPY.modes through a single `.modeCard`. Two JSX blocks
     side by side are two blocks somebody edits one of.
  3. NO SELECTOR HOOK. application.module.scss carries no modifier and no
     :first-child / :last-child anywhere.
  4. ONE GLYPH COMPONENT, aria-hidden, used six times.

WHAT IS ASSERTED, in a browser:
  - exactly two cards, counted against APPLICATION_COPY.modes.length
  - identical rendered width and height (R148's numbers, not a glance)
  - three lines each, a glyph on every one
  - ONE distinct path `d` across all six, and ONE computed colour
  - no element in either card whose class or attributes match
    warn|caution|danger|alert|risk|recommend
  - no "recommand" or "conseill" anywhere in the pair

WHAT A READER WOULD SEE IF IT DRIFTED. The cheapest drift is the third
line of the automatic card - the background-location requirement -
rendered amber with a triangle instead of green with a check. Nothing
else would change. The page would still read as balanced and would have
made the choice: manual becomes the safe option, automatic the one you
were warned about, and the reader never notices they were steered. That
is the highlighted middle column of a pricing table, dressed as candour.
The single-colour assertion is what makes it impossible to ship quietly.


THE READ-THROUGH
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Pointed at one question, as the prompt directs: DO THE TWO CARDS TAKE A
SIDE? Read as somebody deciding whether to let an application watch where
they are.

THE ANSWER IS NO, and the reason is that the automatic card's third line
is written in the same register as the manual card's second:

  manual     "Aucune autorisation a accorder"
  automatic  "Necessitera l'acces a la position, y compris en
              arriere-plan"

Both are flat statements of what the mode needs. Neither is hedged,
apologised for, or softened. A reader who wants to avoid background
location finds that out in one line and picks manual; a reader who wants
the day proposed to them finds out what it costs in one line and picks
automatic. The page does not have an opinion about which of those readers
is right.

WHAT THE READ-THROUGH FOUND, AND IT IS IN MY OWN WORK. The cards were in
the PRESENT TENSE on a page whose lead says the application does not
exist and whose refusals band is entirely in the future:

  lead       "Cette application n'existe pas encore"
  refusals   "Elle n'enregistrera aucune coordonnee"
  card A     "Un geste par jour, et la journee EST ENREGISTREE"
  card B     "L'application COMPARE votre position ... et vous PROPOSE"
  card A     "FONCTIONNE hors connexion"

That is the defect lib/fr/releve-annuel.ts argues against in its own
comment - half-derived is worse than not derived, because a lead saying
one thing above a section saying another is R172 inside a single page. I
wrote that argument in increment 2 and shipped the thing it forbids in
increment 4.

AND MY OWN DETECTOR DID NOT CATCH IT, THREE TIMES, each a different way
of proving nothing:

  1. tests/support/present-tense.ts was a list of PHRASES written from
     what I imagined a shipped-app page saying rather than from what my
     page said. It carried "...compare votre position a la frontiere et
     ENREGISTRE"; the page said "compare ... et vous PROPOSE".
  2. rewritten to match VERBS, one pattern ended with a \b after an
     accented letter - the ASCII word-boundary trap, which matches
     nothing at all. I WROTE THE COMMENT EXPLAINING THAT TRAP IN
     tests/support/product-claims.ts TWO INCREMENTS EARLIER and set it
     again in the same task.
  3. the "Fonctionne hors connexion" pattern used ^ with no m flag. It
     fired on the bare sample string at position 0 - which is how the
     non-vacuity check was written - and matched nothing on the served
     page, where that line sits mid-document. THE SAMPLE HAD BEEN SHAPED
     TO THE PATTERN'S CONVENIENCE.

Only a planted present-tense line ON THE REAL PAGE caught the third, and
R180 is why it was trusted: the plant was verified to have changed the
file before its result was read.

FIXED: the mode bodies and every fact line are now functions of appExists
like the rest of the page; the detector matches verbs; and the spec
asserts BOTH directions - it fires on the five forms the page actually
carried before the read-through, and stays silent on the ten future forms

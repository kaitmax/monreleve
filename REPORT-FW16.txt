ANCHOR STABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
An anchor is a URL: people link to #comment-le-pourcentage-se-calcule from
support replies and their own notes, so an anchor that changes is a link
that breaks silently for everybody who saved it.

THE SLUG RULE IS OURS rather than rehype-slug's, so it cannot move with
somebody else's minor version. Tested against the cases this subject
produces - accents, percentages, decimal commas, guillemets.

  'Ce que dit l(U+2019)accord'        -> ce-que-dit-l-accord
  'Le seuil social de 49,9 %'         -> le-seuil-social-de-49-9
  'Si vous depassez 40 %'             -> si-vous-depassez-40
  '  << Les jours de mission >>  '    -> les-jours-de-mission

THE REBUILD COMPARISON: identical source yields identical ids, asserted
twice in a row AND against a written-out expected list, so the test cannot
pass by comparing a function to itself. Two headings with the same text
are disambiguated BY POSITION - a random suffix would move on every
rebuild, which is the one thing an anchor must never do.

FAQ IDENTITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
One array, two consumers. The accordion a reader opens and the FAQPage a
crawler reads are built from frontmatter.faq; the id is DERIVED with the
same slug rule the headings use, because an id in frontmatter would be a
URL fragment written by hand.

ASSERTED FROM THE SERVED PAGE: the questions and answers are read back out
of the ld+json script and compared against the rendered <summary> and <p>
elements. Equal, both lists, on the real HTML.

AND THE PROPERTY IT RESTS ON, asserted separately: every answer is in the
DOM while the accordion is CLOSED. A collapsed accordion that did not
render its answers would publish claims nobody can see - cloaking arrived
at by accident.

An article with no FAQ emits NO FAQPage at all, rather than an empty
mainEntity.

DEVIATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ARTICLE HEAD WAS MISALIGNED WITH ITS OWN PROSE, and a baseline is
   what caught it.

     head  left 472           prose  left 572, width 616
     after: both left 472, both width 680

   Container CENTRES its measure and PADS it - right for a page-level
   column, wrong inside a grid track, where it also cut FD13's 680 px
   measure to 616. NOTHING IN THE SUITE COULD SEE IT: 2246 unit tests,
   137 e2e assertions and 86 baselines, every element present, styled and
   individually correct, because no assertion compared two elements' LEFT
   EDGES. It took cropping the picture and reading it, which is what
   CLAUDE.md says about R135 in the operator's own words.

   The grid track is now the measure itself, so one number decides the
   head's width, the prose's width and their shared left edge. The e2e
   locks all three plus the 280 px column.

2. THE CLOSING CARD LINKS THE CALCULATOR, NOT /releve-annuel, and FD13
   contradicts itself here. Its rules say "exactly two calculator CTAs:
   the inline card mid-article and the closing card"; its example copy
   for that card says "Decouvrir le releve annuel". SPEC 9.5 settles it -
   every guide links a calculator twice - and the prompt says SPEC wins.
   It is also the only reading that works: /releve-annuel is planned for
   FW20, so the other one links a page that 404s and the mesh checker
   refuses it.

3. THE CLOSING CARD USES h2 WHERE FD13 SAYS h3. The article's sections
   are h2; a closing card at h3 would read as a subsection of the last
   section rather than as a peer. The heading-order assertion in
   accessibility-guides.test.tsx is what made the choice explicit.

4. /guides STAYS `planned` IN THE ROUTE REGISTRY. The route exists - the
   breadcrumb points at it and the mesh checker needs a hub - but with
   zero published guides, listing it in the sitemap would tell a crawler
   a page exists that says nothing: the registry's own "sitemap of 404s"
   principle one level down. A test asserts the hub is EMPTY today, so
   whoever adds the first article gets a failure telling them to flip the
   flag deliberately.

5. THE WARNING BANNER'S ACCENT USES --size-input-border-focus, which is
   not what it is for, and this is the SIXTH site to do it - the
   calculator's three notices, the result panel's and /sources' all draw
   their left accent from the input focus border. FD13 and
   DESIGN-SYSTEM.md both say 4 px; that token is 2 px; no accent token
   exists. Matching the five was right here - a 4 px accent on guides and
   2 px everywhere else would be an inconsistency I introduced - but this
   is R69's exact shape. See QUESTIONS.

6. THE FIXED CAP ON THE ISLAND-HARNESS EXCLUSION LIST WAS REMOVED. It
   was two; FW15 raised it to three and four; FW16 arrived with
   TableOfContents and GuideCta. Each raise was correct alone and the
   sequence is the erosion a ratchet exists to prevent - which is the
   signal that the NUMBER tracked how fast the library grows rather than
   whether the harness still measures it. Replaced by two strictly
   stronger assertions: exclusions must stay under half the library, and
   every exclusion's stated reason is CHECKED AGAINST ITS SOURCE ('use
   client' present for PublicShell, absent for the five server
   components). Nothing slips through the pair that the count caught.

4. THE FIXTURE'S READING TIME IS "2 min de lecture" for a short article.

   real guide has exercised it yet.

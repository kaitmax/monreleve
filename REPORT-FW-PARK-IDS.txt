THE DEFECT IS LARGER THAN THE ONE THE PROMPT DESCRIBES, and the extra half was
found by measuring rather than by reading the prompt.

`PARKAN?` is not "PARK, then optionally AN". It is the five letters PARKA
followed by an optional N. So `/\bPARKAN?-\d+\b/g` matched PARKAN-<n> and
PARKA-<n>:

    "PARK-12"        ->  null
    "PARKAN-45"      ->  ["PARKAN-45"]
    "PARKA-7"        ->  ["PARKA-7"]
    "PARK-K6"        ->  null

The lettered ids were invisible, as the prompt says. So was the ENTIRE PARK-<n>
series - twenty-four entries, nineteen of them cited by a report, across
twelve reports. The headline the check
has printed since R327, "register coverage: OK - 39 cited id(s) across 115
report(s)", was thirty-nine PARKAN-<n> ids and nothing else. And the pattern
was simultaneously too WIDE: PARKA-<n> is a shape the census contains zero
instances of, and the check would have accepted one.

The same expression sat a second time in the heading scan at line 137, so a
"Not transcribed" placeholder under `## PARK-12 -` or `## PARK-K6 -` could not
be flagged either: it saw 39 of parking.md's 97 headings.

Two commits:

  122e1dd  the 28 lettered entries, back-filled into parking.md
  78ece08  the widened pattern, one home for it, and the test that
           demonstrates the gap

THE BACK-FILL COMES FIRST, which reverses the prompt's step order on purpose:
widening before back-filling leaves the register check RED for the length of
one commit on both gates that run it, and a red commit is a bounce. R182's
shape - a check runs after the thing it checks for can exist.

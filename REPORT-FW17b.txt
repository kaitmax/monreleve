WHY EACH QUESTIONABLE ENTRY IS ON THE LIST. Every form is one that no
correct French word spells that way. The list is CURATED because deriving
it does not work: stripping accents off the articles' own vocabulary
proposes votre, des, sur, compte, entre, note, passe, donne, demande,
compare, tranche, copie, valide, and the verb forms horodate ("il
horodate") and genevoise - 117 occurrences, every one correct French. A
guard reporting those is a guard somebody deletes. A test asserts they
are NOT reported, so the day one is added the suite says so.

THE DISCRIMINATOR, and it is a rule rather than an exemption list. Prose
contains a space; an identifier does not. That removes "/releve-annuel",
"regime-lost", "NEGATIVE_DAYS", "/confidentialite" and every slug and
href in the articles, without an entry that has to grow with each new
route, enum member or slug. SPEC 14 makes slugs permanent, so they are
permanently unaccented, and a guard that flagged them would be refusing
something that cannot change.

Three further exclusions were needed and each is stated in the file:
 - MDX comments, which are English commentary (R91's and R147's
   treatment);
 - lines that introduce a quoted value, which are scanned by their quoted
   spans instead - `regime: "Cantons de l'accord de 1983"` has an object
   KEY that is never rendered and a STRING that is;
 - the frontmatter fields that hold identifiers - slug, cluster,
   related - excluded BY CONTRACT rather than by shape. The tempting
   rule, "a hyphenated lowercase token is a slug", would swallow
   mi-temps, au-dela, demi-journee and compte-t-elle.

THE OTHER DIRECTION is asserted too: a codepoint count over lib/fr's
quoted spans, floored at 850 (it is 885 today). Somebody facing a failure
could "fix" it by deleting the accented word or rewording around it; the
count would collapse and the test fails. Same shape as copy-codepoints'
apostrophe count.

THE GUARD IS A FLOOR, NOT A CEILING, and reading proved it in the same
sitting. Two defects it could not report:
  'Guides lies'            -> 'Guides lies' with its accent
  'Les questions precises' -> 'Les questions precises' with its accent
`precises` was unambiguous and is now on the list. `lies` CANNOT BE:
"tu lies" is correct French, so that form belongs to reading rather than
to a scan. Recorded rather than forced onto the list.

BREADCRUMB (Q45)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
`breadcrumbLabel` in lib/guides/frontmatter.ts. REQUIRED and capped at 40
characters: optional would mean falling back to the title, which is the
defect with a default, and the cap is what makes it a label rather than a
second title. Not permanent the way a slug is - SPEC 14 governs URLs and
this is presentation - but it changes what a reader meets inside a
navigation landmark.

R147 WAS DECIDED RATHER THAN EXEMPTED. FD13's example carries the figure
("Regle des 40 %"), which is the collision the title had in FW17. The
title and slug are exempt as identity fields, and the comment that made
that exemption says a THIRD field wanting one is a ruling, not an edit.
So the labels name the rule:

  Le plafond fiscal        teletravail-frontalier-40-pourcent
  Le seuil social          seuil-social-49-9-pourcent
  Les jours de mission     jours-de-mission-hors-suisse
  Article de demonstration the fixture

It costs nothing: a breadcrumb is not a search surface and the slug still
carries the term. This is R153's shape one field along.

THE FIELD IS ASSERTED TO MEAN SOMETHING. hub.test.ts asserts, for every
article, that breadcrumbLabel is NOT the title and is shorter than it - a
label somebody fills by copying the title is the field not existing.

Rendered and read: "Guides / Le plafond fiscal".

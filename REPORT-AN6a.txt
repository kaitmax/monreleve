--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT AN6a PROVES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AN6a proves that the CLASSIFIER does what it was designed to do: seven synthetic
tracks, each a scenario with its expected proposal written before it ran (R197),
are replayed through the real sampling entry point (DetectionSession.onFix) on
the emulator and in the JVM regression, and each produces exactly its expected
tier and proposal. The border geometry, the buffer, the hysteresis fold, the
tally, the tier thresholds and the proposal rule are all exercised end to end,
and no coordinate survives its classification (proven three ways, each with a
planted violation confirmed to fire).

AN6a does NOT prove the design is right for REAL signals. A synthetic track is
whatever coordinates it is fed; it cannot tell you how a phone behaves in
Annemasse, in a Leman Express tunnel, or on a Xiaomi that kills background work.
Whether the thresholds are the right ones, whether a real suburban border
produces the tallies these tracks assume, and what a working day actually costs
in battery -- those are AN6b, a person carrying the phone across the border for
weeks, scored by the harness this task delivers. A green AN6a is NOT a pass at
Checkpoint G. It is the instrument that makes Checkpoint G measurable, scoreable,
and honest.

R275 -- A COALESCED MUTATION CARRIES THE FINAL TAP'S CLAIM TIME (increment 0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Carried from AN5's Q2. The ruling: a coalesced mutation's clientClaimedAt must
be the tap that set its FINAL value, because a timestamp describes the value it
travels with. AN5's report did not say which tap the code kept; this closes that
with a test.

Test (EntryRepositoryTest.aCoalescedEditCarriesTheFinalTapsClaimTime, bf88b54):
record HOME in the morning (07:00 UTC = 09:00 Paris), correct to CH that evening
(15:00 UTC = 17:00 Paris), both offline so they coalesce onto one (dayDate,
portion) row. Assert the coalesced row's clientClaimedAt is the EVENING tap
(2027-06-18T17:00:00.000+02:00), NOT the morning one (09:00) -- claiming CH was
recorded at 09:00, when the day held HOME then, would be a false claim the
evidence model refuses.

RED first: with clientClaimedAt = existing?.clientClaimedAt ?: deviceClaimNow()
(keep the original tap), the assertion failed on 09:00. GREEN with
clientClaimedAt = deviceClaimNow(clock) recomputed at every record(), so the
last tap's time is the one stored. The immutable server columns (firstRecordedAt,
serverId) still survive the edit; only the client's claim-of-value time moves,
which is correct: that field describes the value, and the value changed.

THE POLYGON (SPEC 22 open question 9, answered)

Source integrity recorded in docs/detection-model.md: 1,197,323 bytes,
sha256 cb41591c...78c0. The 1.2 MB raw source is not committed (re-fetchable,

LICENCE, READ FROM THE SOURCE (R146). OpenStreetMap data is under the Open Data
Commons Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0, read from openstreetmap.org/copyright
on the fetch date (it names the ODbL and links the legal code at
opendatacommons.org). Two real obligations follow, both recorded as NEEDS:
  - Attribution: the product must credit "(c) OpenStreetMap contributors"
    wherever the border data is used. No such screen exists yet -- a NEEDS for

  - Share-alike: the bundled and reference polygons are a derived database; if
    the product ever publicly conveys them AS DATA, that database is ODbL 1.0.

SIMPLIFICATION TOLERANCE AND ITS TEST AGAINST THE BUFFER. Douglas-Peucker at
25 m -- chosen WELL BELOW the 150 m buffer (D25), so simplification can never
move the border far enough to misclassify a point the buffer exists to protect.
BorderPolygonTest.simplificationErrorStaysFarBelowTheBuffer measures the bundled
polygon against the UNSIMPLIFIED Geneva reference at 50/100/200/500 m offsets
either side of the real border and asserts the error stays far below the buffer.
A polygon simplified at 200 m would fail that test -- which is the point of
stating the tolerance rather than trusting it.

SIZE. Bundled: app/src/main/assets/border/switzerland.json, GeoJSON MultiPolygon,
6,878 points (from 52,159), 157,852 bytes (~154 KB), sha256 11d1534f...b400.

6,171 points. The runtime geometry (detection/BorderPolygon.kt) is pure Kotlin,
no geo dependency (R136).

ENCLAVE LIMITATION, RECORDED HONESTLY. This extract returns the outer boundary
with no interior rings, so Buesingen (DE) and Campione (IT) are not cut out; a
point inside either would classify as INSIDE. Both sit on the German and Italian
borders, far from the Franco-Swiss border near Geneva this product serves, so it
is immaterial today. The ray-cast is nonetheless hole-aware (it processes
interior rings), so a hole-carrying source drops in with no code change. A NEEDS
only if enclave accuracy ever matters.

THE COORDINATE'S LIFETIME (AC2 -- three proofs, each with a landed plant)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D18/R277: no coordinate is stored, logged, transmitted or retained beyond its
classification. A fix enters classify(lat, lng), leaves as a Classification
(INSIDE/OUTSIDE/BUFFER) plus its epoch millis, and the coordinate is discarded.
Proven three independent ways, each with a violation planted and confirmed to
fire (R178/R180):

1. SOURCE FENCE (FencesTest, fence 2b, AN1's re-proven). No latitude/longitude
   type or field name appears in source OUTSIDE detection/. The three synthetic
   coordinates and the TrackPoint that names them live in detection/, which the
   fence permits. PLANT: a latitude field added to an entity outside detection/
   -> fence 2b RED. Reverted.

2. ROOM DDL SCAN (RoomSchemaContractTest, AN5's re-proven, now iterating every
   table). noColumnIsCoordinateShapedInAnyTable walks both entities (entry and
   the new daily_tally) and fails on any column whose name is coordinate-shaped.
   daily_tally has none -- it is minutes, counts, a window and a tier. PLANT: a
   latitude column on daily_tally -> the DDL scan RED. Reverted; schema hash
   restored.

3. RUNTIME TYPE ASSERTION (CoordinateConfinementTest). ClassifiedFix, the
   classifier's OUTPUT type, is reflected over and asserted to carry no
   coordinate field -- only a Classification and epochMillis. (Synthetic and
   compiler-generated fields -- jacoco's $jacocoData, Compose's $stable -- are
   filtered by isSynthetic || name starts with "$", so the assertion measures
   the declared shape.) PLANT: a latitude property on ClassifiedFix -> the
   reflection assertion RED. Reverted.

The measurement build (AN6b) carries D18 UNCHANGED (R277): failures are diagnosed
from the TALLY -- minutes each side, fix count, buffer-fix count, tier -- which
is exactly what production stores. The export the scorer reads carries no
coordinate key (FieldExportTest.theExportCarriesNoCoordinate). The privacy model
survives the one task most tempted to break it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HYSTERESIS AND THE TALLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HYSTERESIS (D26), in detection/Tally.kt. A change of side is provisional until it
has persisted 10 minutes (HYSTERESIS_PROVISIONAL_MINUTES) and significant after
30 (HYSTERESIS_SIGNIFICANT_MINUTES). Excursions shorter than 10 minutes fold back
into the preceding side -- the GPS-flicker guard for someone living near the
border. TallyComputerTest proves the boundary (9 min folds, 10/11 min stand),
oscillation folding, and that buffer time is attributed to neither side.

MINUTES COME FROM THE FIXES' OWN TIMESTAMPS, never the wall clock: the gap after
each fix is attributed to its side. This is what lets a compressed replay of a
day produce the tally a real day would -- the tunnel-gap track (below) depends
on it.

THE WORKING WINDOW. 07:00-19:00 Europe/Paris, pinned to Paris regardless of the
device timezone (R28, WorkingWindowTest), stored on each tally as minutes-of-day
so a past tally keeps the window it was computed under.

THE TABLE (daily_tally, Room v2). Per day: minutesSwitzerland, minutesElsewhere,
fixCount, bufferFixCount, the window, and tier (nullable). No coordinate column.
MIGRATION_1_2 creates it; MonReleveMigrationTest runs the v1->v2 migration on the
emulator and validates it against schemas/2.json -- the migration harness's first
real execution.

TIERS (SPEC 22 open question 7 -- encoded, not chosen)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THRESHOLDS ARE ENCODED FROM docs/field-protocol-AN6b.md SECTION 3 (R276), in
detection/Proposal.kt. This task does NOT choose, adjust or calibrate them --
picking a threshold after seeing field data is fitting the gate to the
measurement. TierThresholdParityTest reads the six numbers back out of that
document and fails if the constants drift (R181), so the doc is the source and
the code follows it:

  HIGH:   samples >= 50% of expected, buffer <= 20%, winning side >= 85%
  MEDIUM: samples >= 25% of expected, buffer <= 40%, winning side >= 70%
  LOW:    anything else -- and LOW WITHHOLDS (no proposal; the day stays
          Non renseigne, entered by hand).

"Expected samples" is the window length over the sampling cadence
(SAMPLE_INTERVAL_MINUTES = 15, SPEC 13.1's WorkManager floor): a 12 h window
expects 48. The proposal proposes only the winning side (INSIDE -> En Suisse / CH,
OUTSIDE -> Domicile / HOME); > 4 h is a full day, 2-4 h a half day (AM/PM from
where the winning side's fixes fall), under 2 h is withheld. MISSIONS AND ABSENCES
ARE NEVER DETECTED (ProposalTest asserts no tally yields MISSION_FR/MISSION_THIRD/
ABSENT) -- a trip abroad is a business fact, not a geographic one. The tier is
words plus segments, never a percentage (R27). Nothing here writes an entry
(D16): a Proposal is a value; only the confirming tap records it (FencesTest
fence 7, detection/ never references the entry-write path).

THE SEVEN TRACKS (AC1 -- expected written first, observed, seen)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each track's expected result was hand-reasoned from the algorithm and written in
SyntheticTracks.kt BEFORE it ran (R197). All seven were replayed through the real
sampling path (DetectionSession.onFix) two ways: on the emulator by the dev
replay screen (loading the shipped asset polygon), and in the JVM regression by
DetectionSessionTest (in the 643-test gate). Every expected == observed == OK.
The emulator run was RE-CAPTURED AT THIS SESSION'S HEAD (screenshot
an6a-tracks-head.png, in android/build/emulator-shots/, gitignored) because
increment 6 refactored Proposal.kt AFTER increment 5's original observation --
the tracks exercise exactly the refactored proposedKind, so the evidence had to
be photographed on the tree that ships (R184: input, not only output).

  a. commute (home in France, day in Geneva)
     expected HIGH, CH, FULL  -- observed HIGH, CH, FULL  -- OK
     Geneva is ~92% of attributed minutes.
  b. telework (home all day)
     expected HIGH, HOME, FULL -- observed HIGH, HOME, FULL -- OK
     100% one side.
  c. home 120 m from the border (every fix in the buffer)
     expected withheld -- observed withheld (aucune proposition) -- OK
     Every fix is BUFFER; nothing is attributed to either side -> LOW.
  d. tunnel gap (45 min with no fix mid-commute)
     expected HIGH, CH, FULL -- observed HIGH, CH, FULL -- OK
     The gap folds onto the Geneva side; still ~94%. This is the case that
     proves minutes come from the fixes' timestamps, not fix counts.
  e. morning home, afternoon in Geneva (the half-day case)
     expected withheld -- observed withheld -- OK
     ~52% one side, below the 70% floor -> a split day, LOW, withheld rather
     than guessed.
  f. a day with no fixes at all
     expected withheld -- observed withheld -- OK
     Nothing sampled -> LOW.
  g. train crossing that dwells at the border station
     expected withheld -- observed withheld -- OK
     The border dwell pushes buffer > 40% -> LOW.

The emulator screen shows the tier in words (HIGH), never a percentage, and no
map. "Position actuelle: aucune" confirms the OS-location READ layer returns null
on the headless emulator without an active updates client -- see DEVIATIONS.

THE SCORING HARNESS (AC3 -- a worked example, with confidence bounds)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
android/scripts/score-field-run.mjs scores a run mechanically:
  node score-field-run.mjs --export <app export.json> --log <tester log.csv>
                           [--battery-median <pct>]

It reads the app's export (detection/exportFieldRun -- per day: tier, proposed
kind derived by the SAME proposedKind the app uses, minutes and counts; NO
coordinate) and the tester's daily log (date, Suisse/Domicile/Mission/Absent,
note), and prints the R276 verdict per kill criterion: the count, the rate, and
its 95% confidence bound. The thresholds are the field protocol's, encoded
(R276), not chosen. FALSE-CH is on its own line (K1, R278): proposing "En Suisse"
on a home day UNDERSTATES telework -- the person believes they have more headroom
than they do -- so it is scored separately and more strictly (threshold: zero)
than any other error.

THE BOUND IS HONEST, AND SAYS SO. With 20 working days and zero errors the 95%
upper bound on the error rate is ~15% (the rule of three, 3/n). The harness
prints that beside every rate so a good month cannot be read as an accuracy
claim; its final line says "a PASS means not visibly broken, never proof". The
rule of three saturates below n=3 (3/2 = 1.5), so the bound is capped at 100% --
a probability bound above 100% is meaningless, and the honest reading at n=2 is
"this sample says almost nothing", which the capped 100% conveys. (This cap was
the R273 correction above.)

WORKED EXAMPLE (fabricated data, android/scripts/fixtures/). 22 days: 14 HIGH/CH,
3 HIGH/HOME, 2 MEDIUM/CH, 3 withheld. The tester log agrees on every proposed
day, includes two degraded days (train + tunnel) the detector rightly dropped to
MEDIUM, one worked day the phone spent at home in the morning that the detector
WITHHELD (an honest coverage miss, not a wrong guess), and two conge days
(Absent, never detected). Output (verbatim, --battery-median omitted):

  === field run score (R276) ===
  days matched: 22   working days: 20
  a PASS means "not visibly broken", never proof - read the bounds.

    PASS  K1  FALSE-CH on a HIGH day (proposed En Suisse, was at home)
          0 of 17 HIGH days   (threshold: zero)   95% upper bound 17.6%
    PASS  K2  Wrong HIGH proposals (any kind)
          0 of 17 HIGH days   (threshold: at most 1)   95% upper bound 17.6%
    PASS  K3  Wrong MEDIUM proposals
          0 of 2 MEDIUM days (0.0%)   (threshold: at most 15.0%)   95% upper bound 100.0%
    PASS  K4  Coverage (HIGH or MEDIUM on a working day)
          19 of 20 working days (95.0%)   (threshold: at least 60.0%)
    n/a   K5  Battery, median per working day
          measured separately (profiler method)   (threshold: at most 3%)
    PASS  K6  A day written without the person tap
          measurement mode never writes an entry (D16, fence 7)   (threshold: never)

  Checkpoint G, so far: no criterion failed (not a pass - see the bounds) (K5 not measured this run)

Read the bounds, not the PASSes: K1/K2 are certain of nothing tighter than ~18%
error on 17 HIGH days, and K3 with two MEDIUM days is certain of nothing at all
(bound 100.0%). That is what 22 fabricated days buy; a real month buys more, and
AN6b's job is to spend enough days that the bounds mean something. With
--battery-median 2.4 the K5 line reads PASS 2.4% (threshold at most 3%) and the
"K5 not measured" note drops.

FieldExportTest proves the export the scorer eats: proposedKind matches what the
app would propose, the key set is exactly the seven the scorer reads, and there
is no coordinate-shaped key.

BATTERY METHOD (R233 -- the method matters as much as the figure)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 3%-per-working-day budget (K5) is a NUMBER THAT MUST BE MEASURED. From the
android-profiler notes (docs/detection-model.md records it in full):
  - Preferred: a Perfetto system trace over a working-day background window.
    Energy from the power_rails track (power_ma * duration, summed, mWh);
    confirm the device SUSPENDS between wakes via suspend_state; root-cause any
    failure to suspend via kernel_wakelock -- the budget is dominated by whether
    the device stays asleep between fixes.
  - Hard constraint: power_rails needs on-device power measurement (ODPM), a
    Pixel 6+ (Tensor) or similar. THE 3% FIGURE CANNOT BE MEASURED ON AN
    EMULATOR (no power rails), so AN6a does not produce it. AN6b measures it on
    the tester's physical phone, the same way every day, and reports it to
    score-field-run.mjs --battery-median.
  - Fallback where ODPM is absent: dumpsys batterystats (coarser), reset at the
    window start, read at the end.

WHAT AN6b NEEDS FROM THE OPERATOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RATIFY THE SECTION-3 THRESHOLDS BEFORE DAY ONE, THEN FREEZE THEM. The six
   tier numbers and the six kill criteria in docs/field-protocol-AN6b.md are
   encoded exactly as written. If any is wrong, it must change in the DOCUMENT
   before AN6b begins -- TierThresholdParityTest will then fail until the code
   is re-encoded, which is the intended tripwire. Changing a threshold after
   field data exists is refused (R276).
2. TESTERS WHO TELEWORK, ON PHONES THAT FIGHT BACKGROUND WORK. Each tester must
   telework >= 5 days in the window (so there are home days to get wrong), and
   the fleet must include the phone brands that kill background apps (Xiaomi,
   Huawei) -- an accuracy result on a Pixel says nothing about a Xiaomi. This
   is the longest-lead item; recruiting is the critical path, not the code.
3. A physical Pixel 6+ (or ODPM device) for the battery figure; an emulator
   cannot produce K5.
4. AN OSM/ODbL ATTRIBUTION LINE in the eventual detection UI (AN8), and a
   decision on whether the polygon is ever conveyed as data (share-alike).
5. The measurement build runs WITHOUT A SERVER (detection is on-device, D18) and
   is BLIND (it does not show the tester its proposals during the run; the export
   carries them at the end), so the daily log stays independent ground truth.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTICED BUT NOT TOUCHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The scorer's parseLog splits the tester CSV on comma and reads only the first
  two fields, so a note containing commas ("train, tunnel") is tolerated but its
  tail is dropped. Fine for scoring (only date and actual matter), but a note
  with a comma is silently truncated in any future note-aware use. Not touched --
  the scorer does not read the note.
- The dev replay screen's onCreate runs all seven replays synchronously, which
  is ~15 s on a cold-booted emulator and shows a splash first. Acceptable for a
  dev-only screen; a production detection UI (AN8) would not replay on the main
  thread.

QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
None blocking. One for the operator's judgement, not a decision this task may
take: the worked example shows coverage 95% (19/20) with one worked day withheld
because the phone was at home in the morning. K4's floor is 60%, so this passes
comfortably -- but the field protocol treats coverage and accuracy as separate
criteria, and a real fleet on background-hostile phones may trade coverage for
accuracy (fewer proposals, but the ones made are right). That is exactly what
AN6b measures; flagging it here so the coverage/accuracy trade-off is read as
expected, not as a surprise, when the first real numbers land.

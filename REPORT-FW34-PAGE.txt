EACH SECTION AGAINST THE DESIGN
====================================================================
The page is app/(public)/page.tsx (a SERVER component), with the phone card in
app/(public)/HeroPhone.tsx, copy in lib/fr/home.ts, the fixture and guide slugs
in lib/content/home.ts, and styles in app/(public)/home.module.scss.

HEADER + BURGER. FW33's shell header/footer, unchanged (Q1). The home renders no
header of its own. The no-JS burger the relay describes is NOT built: adding it
would edit the shared shell and regenerate every page's baseline, which Q1
forbids. The shell already provides a phone-width burger (JS-driven, shipped in
FW33); it is kept as-is. Recorded under DEVIATIONS.

HERO. Built as designed. The phone card is RENDERED, not pictured: HeroPhone
composes the site's own ZoneBadge, RateDisplay, ThresholdRail and two MetricTiles,
fed by calculateTelework() over lib/content/home.ts's worked example (38 telework
days on the 240-day simplified basis, GE, full time, whole year, no missions).
The three figures - 15,83 % (declarable rate), 58 (days before the fiscal
ceiling) and 81,5 (days before the social ceiling) - come FROM the engine and
are never typed (R147, R163); a unit test ties them to calculateTelework over the
same fixture. The caption interpolates the example's days and the basis from the
fixture and the RuleSet. The rail's tick labels anchor outward (R39, inherited
from the real component).
  - DEVIATION from the mock, recorded: RateDisplay is given NO rawRate, so the
    hero shows the single headline rate (the mock shows one rate). For 38/240 the
    raw rate (15,9) diverges from the declarable (15,83); the full calculator, a
    tap away, shows both. RateDisplay's own contract allows withholding rawRate.
  - The real ThresholdRail (compact) renders a small rate caption above the bar
    (the mock omitted it); accepted as the cost of using the real component.

CALCULATOR. A plain HTML <form method="get" action="/calculateur-teletravail-
frontalier">. It works with no JavaScript and adds no client code: a submit
navigates to the calculator with the scenario in the query, which the
calculator's island reads on mount. "Vous habitez en France et travaillez en
Suisse" is a SENTENCE, not a choice (R282). The three fields map to the
calculator's EXISTING params (lib/params/schema.ts), none invented or renamed
(R156): canton -> `canton`, "Jours teletravailles depuis le 1er janvier" ->
`tt`, "Jours de mission, en France ou ailleurs" -> `mfr`.
  - THE MISSION FIELD maps to `mfr` (missions France). The design merges France
    and third-state missions into ONE field; the engine keeps them separate (two
    params, imputed France-first in the waterfall). The home form is a quick
    start that pre-fills the full calculator, where the split is refined, and the
    figures the hero emphasises (the rate, the headroom) are identical to the
    split while missions stay within budget. No field is parked - every field has
    an existing param. Recorded as the mfr interpretation.
  - The select lists all 26 cantons (lib/fr/cantons CANTONS_BY_NAME), not the
    mock's six, for correctness and consistency with the full calculator.
  - inputmode="numeric" and whole-day defaults; a comma decimal is a full-
    calculator case (that form normalises it client-side). Minor, recorded.

THE TWO RULES. Each card's sentences are tied to verified REG-APPLY rows and
each cites its source as the mock does. 40 % and 49,9 % (and the 25 % band and
the 10-day cap) come from the RuleSet through guideScope(), NEVER typed (R147);
typed-numbers.test.ts refuses a typed figure in lib/fr. The fiscal card cites
"AFC Geneve ; accord amiable franco-suisse du 29 avril 2026" and the social card
cites "Office federal des assurances sociales (OFAS)" - both are VERIFIED rows in
docs/regulatory-sources.md (the accord of 29 April 2026, OFAS/BSV, REG-APPLY
2026-10-11). A unit test asserts the figures are delivered and the cited
instruments are in the verified register. No sentence without a verified row
(R146).

HOW IT WORKS + THE RELEVE. About the app, so FUTURE tense (R138): "Bientot sur
Android", "Chaque releve portera un code de verification", "pourra le scanner".
The releve thumbnail is MARKUP, not an image, with sample data (Camille Martin,

thumbnail is aria-hidden. The verification-code sentence stays future until
FW-VERIFY's QR is switched on. product-claims.test.ts confirms no present-tense
capability claim leaks for the tracker/dossier/app.

PRIVACY. "Nous ne vous suivons pas." Each item is a true "Aucun ..." claim; the

oubliee"). No "100 % securise", no "jamais partagees" - the mock's "ne sont ni

THE LAUNCH SIGN-UP - THE PATH, AND WHY
====================================================================
The design's app section has an email sign-up promising launch news ("le jour du
lancement"). This is NOT built:
  (a) There is NO launch-news list, and REUSING the regulatory alert list is
      refused - its consent is for "alertes reglementaires", a different purpose,
      and consent must name its purpose. A launch-specific list (model +
      endpoint + its own consent) is parked PARK-FW34-LAUNCH.
  (b) The section must not show a form that accepts an address the server cannot
      honour. So no email form is rendered at all; instead the section links to
      /alertes (the existing alerts page), named honestly ("Recevoir les alertes
      reglementaires", "nos alertes par e-mail vous previennent des que nos
      regles changent"), and only while PRODUCT_STATE.alertsExist. A link is
      never shown broken, so the "hide when sending is not configured" concern is
      satisfied structurally - the /alertes page owns its own degraded-delivery
      state (RESEND_API_KEY), not the home.
This DEVIATES from the mock's launch-email copy (recorded); honesty (R138/R172)
and consent require it.

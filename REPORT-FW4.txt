7.3 requires the separation because the rules conflict on purpose.

  raw-rate.ts:37             Math.ceil      round up to 1 dp, on integers

                                             the mission cap both round up"
  denominator.ts:54          Math.ceil      ceil, after a float-hygiene
                                             normalisation (Math.round)
  allowed-days.ts:63         (call site)    "floor to the half-day - advisory,
                                             never overstate (R12)"
  allowed-days.ts:66         (call site)    "ceil - favours the user

  calculate.ts:54            (call site)    "ceil - favours the user

waterfall.ts contains no rounding at all, by design: it is pure day
arithmetic, so no rule can hide in it.

validate.ts:22 uses Math.round inside a granularity-step check. It is not a
rounding of any reported quantity - it asks whether a value lands on a half or
a whole day within 1e-9 - and is noted here only so the inventory is complete.

denominator.ts:54 is the one site with two operations. The Math.round is the
float-hygiene normalisation and the comment states in terms that it is NOT a
regulatory rounding rule; the Math.ceil is the regulatory one.

TWO INDEPENDENT INTEGER-ARITHMETIC DECISIONS, both load-bearing:

declarable-rate.ts and raw-rate.ts do their arithmetic on the numerator and
denominator as integers rather than on a pre-computed percentage. This is not
style. 23/125 is exactly 18.4 %, 18.4 has no exact IEEE 754 representation,
and truncating the float yields 18.39 - biased downward, on the number filed
with the AFC. The 1e6-style normalisation that fixed the FW3 denominator case
is USELESS here, because multiplying ~18.4 by 100 reintroduces the same error.
Measured across every numerator against every denominator up to 500, 125 750
pairs: the float form is wrong in 36, the integer form in none. A 125-day
denominator is employmentRate 52 on the simplified basis, and 51/125 = 40.8 %
sits inside the band that decides whether the regime survives.

R12 - allowedTeleworkDays now FLOORS atFiscal and atSocial to the half-day.

FW3 returned them unrounded, on the reading that SPEC 7.2 step 5 defines
fiscalCeiling without rounding. R12 overrides that: the function answers "how

  denominator 200:  atSocial   99.8 -> 99.5      atFiscal 80 unchanged
  denominator 120:  atFiscal     48 unchanged

Two FW3 tests were updated to the new expectation, each citing the ruling at
the assertion. This is a ruled change, not a regression: the old numbers were
correct under the old reading and are wrong under the new one. The exact
unrounded ceiling remains available as fiscalCeilingDays on TeleworkResult, so
nothing is lost. missionCap still ceils.

R14 - NOT_IMPLEMENTED is absent from ValidationError.

It never existed. FW3's seam returned ok:false with an EMPTY error list rather
than add a code to the public union, precisely because adding one would have
been an ask-first public-API change and would have needed French copy in
lib/fr for a state no user could reach. So R14's removal is a NO-OP on the
type, and the honest thing is to say so rather than report a deletion that did
not happen.

What was added is the assertion that keeps it a no-op:

  type NotImplemented = Extract<ValidationError, { code: 'NOT_IMPLEMENTED' }>;
  expectTypeOf<NotImplemented>().toEqualTypeOf<never>();

A member added later makes Extract resolve to something other than never and
fails typecheck. Two further tests pin that an empty error list can no longer
occur - which was the old sentinel - and that only the six spec codes are
ever emitted.

R11 - RULE_SET_FALLBACK was added to the Warning union.

A public API change, authorised by the ruling. calculateTelework compares
input.taxYear with the applied rule set's taxYear and emits both years, so a
2027 dossier computed under 2026 rules says so rather than leaving a caller to
notice a mismatch it would have to think to check.

QUESTIONS FOR THE OPERATOR
--------------------------

1. WHEN SHOULD A1_REQUIRED FIRE? The one regulatory ambiguity in this task.
   SPEC 7.1 says only that the 49.9 % social ceiling is "conditional on a
   valid A1", and 7.4 lists the code with no condition. I implemented the
   reading that it is a standing obligation rather than a threshold - it fires
   whenever any day is counted against the ceiling - and marked the trigger
   PROVISIONAL at the site. The alternatives I considered and rejected: firing
   only above the fiscal ceiling would make it redundant with
   FISCAL_CEILING_EXCEEDED, since by construction nothing exceeds the ceiling
   in the other branch; firing only near the social ceiling would leave a user
   comfortably under 40 % unaware of a document they are nonetheless required
   to hold. This affects copy, not arithmetic, so no fixture depends on it.

2. THE THRESHOLD GUARD AND DECIMAL-PLACE DIVISORS. no-magic-thresholds
   forbids a bare 10 anywhere in the engine outside rules.ts, because 10 is
   the mission cap. It has now twice caught a power-of-ten divisor instead -
   10 ** 6 in FW3, tenths / 10 here - and both times the fix was to write 1e6
   or 1e1. That works and is arguably clearer, but it is a convention the
   guard is imposing rather than one anyone chose. Options: keep it and write
   the convention down in tasks/plan.md; narrow the guard to flag 10 only
   where it is not a divisor; or drop 10 from the forbidden set and rely on
   the mission cap living in RuleSet. I have no strong preference and have
   changed nothing.

3. SHOULD THE RATE FLOAT CASES BECOME A SHARED FIXTURE? See NOTICED 1. The
   Kotlin port will hit the 23/125 defect unless it inherits a guard, and
   the Geneva six cannot express it. My inclination is a
   fixtures/rate-cases.json mirroring denominator-cases.json, but it extends
   the conformance contract AN2 must satisfy, so it is yours.

4. WHO OWNS THE MISSION-CAP PRORATION UNDER THE EXACT METHOD? Under
   simplified240 the cap prorates off employmentRate and periodFraction,
   which is unambiguous. Under 'exact' the employer supplies a total that may
   not correspond to those figures at all, and the engine still prorates the

PLAY'S VERDICT, FIRST
---------------------
LIKELY REFUSED, as an opt-in alternative to manual entry (with sources + dates in
docs/play-background-location.md).

The reason sits above the mechanics: Google Play permits ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION
only when it is CORE functionality, and this product's own design makes it NOT core.
SPEC and the field protocol state repeatedly that manual mode produces EXACTLY the
same annual statement (SPEC 11.1; D19; the Checkpoint-G fallback is "manual mode
only"), so the Releve - the product's core purpose - is fully delivered without any
location access. Google's policy: "If your app requests access to the location in the
background but it's not important to the app's core functionality, you must remove it"
(support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150, fetched 2026-10-02).
An opt-in alternative to a fully-working manual mode is exactly that case. The 2026
Location Permissions policy update (effective 2026-10-28) makes the verdict firmer:
location must be "central to the app's core purpose."

NOT the problem: (1) Data Safety - Case 1, local-only, EXEMPT, because D18 transmits
no coordinate (docs/android-skills.md 7.2); (2) the restricted-permission mechanics -
a Play Console declaration + a <=30s demo video are producible but do not make the
feature core. The disclosure and in-app wording were designed to meet the
requirements as closely as the feature allows WITHOUT claiming collection the product
does not do. The upload, the Declaration Form and the video are AN14b's; the operator
takes the refusal risk knowingly, and the "manual mode only" path is already the
product's designed fallback, so nothing is lost if review refuses.
